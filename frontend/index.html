How It Works

Understanding the science behind AI-powered drug discovery

01

The Big Picture

Drug discovery traditionally takes 10-15 years and costs $2-3 billion to bring a new drug to market. Our AI system accelerates this by predicting which drug molecules will bind to disease-related proteins.

Drug Molecules

Small chemical compounds that can interact with proteins in the body to treat diseases. We represent them as molecular graphs.

Target Proteins

Large biological molecules made of amino acids. They perform essential functions in cells and are often the targets of drugs.

Binding Affinity

How strongly a drug binds to a protein. Measured in pKd units - higher values mean stronger binding.

02

The Prediction Pipeline

Step 1

Input Processing

Drug molecules are converted from SMILES notation into graphs where atoms are nodes and bonds are edges. Each atom gets a one-hot feature vector (element, degree, formal charge, hydrogen count, hybridization, aromaticity, ring membership).

CC(=O)Nc1ccc(O)cc1 → Graph with 11 atoms, 11 bonds
Step 2

Drug Encoding (GNN)

A Graph Neural Network processes the molecular graph. Each layer aggregates information from neighboring atoms, learning the molecule's structure and properties.

→
128-dim vector
Step 3

Protein Encoding (1D-CNN)

Protein sequences (up to 1,000 residues) are embedded and passed through parallel 1D convolutions with kernel sizes 3, 5 and 7, which detect local amino-acid motifs. Every residue keeps its own embedding so the fusion step can attend to it.

MENFQKVEKIG... → Per-residue embeddings + global protein embedding
Step 4

Cross-Attention Fusion

Cross-attention learns which drug atoms interact with which protein residues. This provides interpretable predictions - we can see where binding occurs!

Drug Atoms
Protein Residues
Step 5

Affinity Prediction

A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) takes the fused representation and outputs a binding affinity score. An ensemble of independently-seeded models gives an uncertainty estimate (their disagreement).

pKd e.g. 7.4
Uncertainty ± ensemble std
03

What You Provide & What You Get

Your Input

Drug SMILES CC(=O)Nc1ccc(O)cc1 A text representation of the drug's chemical structure
Protein Sequence MENFQKVEKIGEGTYGVVYK... The amino acid sequence of the target protein

AI Output

Binding Affinity (pKd) e.g. 7.4 Predicted strength of binding (higher = stronger)
Interaction Type Strong Binder Classification based on affinity threshold
Confidence ± std across the ensemble Agreement between independently trained models plus similarity of the drug to the training set
Binding Site Top attention regions Protein segments receiving the most cross-attention (a hypothesis, not a docked pose)
ADMET Properties Absorption, Toxicity... Rule-based drug-likeness and safety flags computed with RDKit (Lipinski, TPSA, LogP)
04

Model Architecture

Drug Input
SMILES → Graph
Drug Encoder
GIN (graph isomorphism network) 3 layers, 128 dims
Cross-Attention
Atoms → residues 4 heads
Protein Input
Sequence → Embeddings
Protein Encoder
Multi-kernel 1D-CNN kernels 3 / 5 / 7, 128 dims
Prediction Head
MLP 256 → 128 → 1
Output
pKd (seed-ensemble mean ± std)
05

How We Measure Accuracy

CI

Concordance Index

Measures ranking accuracy - if drug A binds stronger than drug B in reality, does the model predict the same ordering?

0.5 = Random
1.0 = Perfect

RMSE

Root Mean Square Error

Average difference between predicted and actual pKd values. Lower is better.

0.0 = Perfect
2.0 = Poor

r

Pearson Correlation

Linear correlation between predicted and actual values. Higher is better.

Data Split Strategies

How we ensure fair model evaluation

Warm Split

Random split of interactions. Both drugs and proteins in test set may appear in training.

Difficulty: Easy

Cold Drug

Test on unseen drug molecules. Tests generalization to new chemical scaffolds.

Difficulty: Medium

Cold Target

Test on unseen protein targets. Tests ability to predict for new disease targets.

Difficulty: Medium

Cold Both

Test on completely unseen drug-protein pairs. The hardest and most realistic scenario.

Difficulty: Hard
